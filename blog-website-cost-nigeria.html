How Much Does a Website Cost in Nigeria in 2026?

"How much does a website cost?" It's one of the first questions businesses ask when they're ready to build online — and there's no single answer.

In Nigeria, you can find someone offering a website for ₦30,000 and another developer quoting ₦2,000,000+. So how do you know what a website should actually cost?

I build websites and digital systems for businesses, and one thing I've learned is that the price isn't really about "a website." It's about what the website needs to do for the business. A simple landing page, a 5-page business website, an online store, and a booking platform are completely different projects.

So in this guide, I'll break down the realistic cost of different types of websites in Nigeria in 2026, what affects the price, what you should ask before paying a developer, and which type of website actually makes sense for your business.

Which type of website does your business actually need?

The cheapest website isn't always the right website. Before asking how much you'll pay, figure out what your business actually needs.

You need a Landing Page if...

You're launching a product, service, campaign, personal brand, or small business and mainly need one focused page that explains what you offer and gets people to take action.

Typical use: Ads → Landing Page → WhatsApp / Call / Form
You need a Business Website if...

You want customers to properly understand your business, explore your services, see your work, and contact you. A typical business website includes Home, About, Services, Portfolio, and Contact pages.

Best for: SMEs, agencies, consultants, service businesses, startups and established brands.
You need an E-Commerce Website if...

You sell products online and want customers to browse products, add items to a cart, make payments and place orders.

Best for: Fashion brands, food businesses, beauty brands, electronics stores and other product-based businesses.
You need a Booking Website if...

Your business depends on customers scheduling appointments or services.

Best for: Salons, clinics, consultants, coaches, photographers and service providers.
You need a Website Redesign if...

You already have a website, but it's outdated, slow, difficult to use, poorly optimized for mobile, or simply isn't converting visitors into customers. Sometimes you don't need a new website — you need a better version of the one you already have.

What are you actually paying for?

When someone gives you a website quote, you're not just paying for pages on a screen. Depending on the project, you're paying for things such as:

  • Strategy and planning
  • UI/UX and visual design
  • Development
  • Mobile responsiveness
  • Forms and integrations
  • SEO setup
  • Payment integration
  • Booking systems
  • Database/backend functionality
  • Domain and hosting setup
  • Testing and bug fixes
  • Revisions
  • Deployment
  • Post-launch support

This is why two developers can quote very different prices for what sounds like "a 5-page website." The important question isn't "Why is this website ₦300,000?" — it's "What exactly am I getting for ₦300,000?"

What actually drives the price up or down

1. Custom design vs. template

A templated site (Wix, a pre-built theme, a page-builder) is cheaper because most of the design decisions are already made. A fully custom design — built from your brand, your content, your goals — takes more hours, but it's the difference between a site that looks like everyone else's and one that actually looks like your business.

2. Number of pages and content

Every extra page is more layout work, more copy to organize, and usually more images to source or shoot. A 3-page site and a 10-page site are simply different jobs.

3. Functionality

Static pages (text, images, a contact form) are the cheapest to build. Anything with logic behind it — a booking calendar, a login system, a shopping cart, a WhatsApp bot integration — needs backend development, which is a different skill set and more time.

4. Hosting and domain (ongoing, not one-time)

A website cost isn't just the build. Budget separately for a domain name (roughly ₦5,000–₦15,000/year depending on the extension) and hosting (free tiers exist on platforms like Netlify for static sites; anything with a backend usually needs a small monthly hosting cost).

5. Who's building it

A student learning to code, an experienced freelancer, and a full agency will all quote differently for the same brief — not because one is "better," but because you're paying for different levels of speed, reliability, and post-launch support.

Questions to ask before you pay for a website

  • Does the price include revisions, or is every change an extra cost?
  • Who owns the domain and hosting account after the project — you or the developer?
  • Is mobile responsiveness included, or is that "extra"? (It shouldn't be — most Nigerian traffic is mobile.)
  • What happens after launch — is there a maintenance retainer, or are you on your own?
  • Can they show live examples of sites they've actually shipped, not just mockups?

Frequently asked questions

How much does a website cost in Nigeria in 2026?
A website can cost anywhere from tens of thousands of naira to several million naira depending on its complexity. A professional landing page, business website, e-commerce store and custom web application all have different requirements and costs.
How much does a 5-page website cost in Nigeria?
A professional 5-page business website can range from around ₦260,000 to ₦500,000 or more depending on design, functionality, SEO, content, integrations and the developer or agency building it.
How much does a website cost for a small business?
A small business may only need a landing page or a simple business website. The right choice depends on whether the main goal is generating inquiries, building credibility, showcasing services or selling products online.
Do I need to pay for hosting and a domain?
Yes. A domain and hosting are separate ongoing costs in most cases. Some platforms offer free hosting for certain types of websites, but businesses should still budget for their domain and any hosting or infrastructure their website requires.
How long does it take to build a business website?
A straightforward business website can often be completed within one to two weeks, while websites involving e-commerce, booking systems, custom functionality or integrations can take longer.
Should I choose a cheap website?
Not necessarily. A low price can be attractive, but you should compare what is actually included: design, responsiveness, SEO, functionality, revisions, ownership, support and maintenance. The cheapest quote isn't always the cheapest option in the long run.

The honest bottom line

If someone quotes you ₦20,000 for a "full business website," ask what exactly that includes — it's usually a single template page with your logo dropped in, not a site built around your business and your customers. On the other end, if an agency quotes ₦3,000,000 for a 4-page brochure site, ask what's driving that number.

The sweet spot for most Nigerian SMEs starting out is a well-built, up-to-5-page site with clear calls to action, fast load times, and mobile-first design — which is exactly where the ₦260,000–₦390,000 Business Website range tends to land. Need something simpler to test an offer first? A single conversion-focused Landing Page starts at ₦130,000.
